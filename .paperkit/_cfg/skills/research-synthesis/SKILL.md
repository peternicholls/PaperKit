# Research Synthesis

## Your Task

When given research materials (notes, PDFs, sources, or raw evidence), transform them into a structured, synthesized document that:

1. **Organizes** evidence by theme, argument, or methodology
2. **Synthesizes** related findings into coherent narratives
3. **Tracks** all sources with proper citations
4. **Identifies** gaps in the research coverage
5. **Maps** evidence to paper sections

---

## Core Process

### Step 1: Inventory Sources

Before synthesizing, catalog what you have:

```
| Source | Type | Key Topics | Quality | Gaps |
|--------|------|------------|---------|------|
| Smith (2023) | Journal | Topic A, B | High | Missing X |
| Jones (2022) | Book | Topic B, C | Medium | Outdated |
```

### Step 2: Extract Evidence

For each source, extract:
- **Direct quotes** (with page numbers)
- **Paraphrased findings** (with attribution)
- **Data/statistics** (with context)
- **Arguments/claims** (with supporting evidence)

### Step 3: Organize by Theme

Group evidence thematically, not by source:

```markdown
## Theme: [Topic Name]

### Key Finding 1
- Evidence from Smith (2023, p. 45): "direct quote..."
- Corroborated by Jones (2022, p. 12): "supporting quote..."
- Contrasted by Brown (2021): different perspective...

### Key Finding 2
...
```

### Step 4: Synthesize

Transform grouped evidence into coherent narrative:

**Before (grouped evidence):**
> Smith says X. Jones says X too. Brown disagrees.

**After (synthesized):**
> The consensus view holds that X (Smith, 2023; Jones, 2022), though Brown (2021) offers a compelling counter-argument based on Y.

### Step 5: Map to Paper Structure

Connect synthesized content to paper sections:

```
| Paper Section | Themes Covered | Evidence Strength | Gaps |
|---------------|----------------|-------------------|------|
| §2 Background | Theme A, B | Strong | None |
| §3 Methods | Theme C | Weak | Need more sources |
| §4 Results | Theme D, E | Medium | Conflicting data |
```

## Synthesis Patterns

### Pattern: Thematic Synthesis

**Use when**: Multiple sources discuss the same topic from different angles.

**Structure**:
1. State the theme/question
2. Present dominant view with evidence
3. Present alternative views
4. Identify points of agreement/disagreement
5. Note implications for your paper

### Pattern: Chronological Synthesis

**Use when**: Tracing the evolution of ideas or research over time.

**Structure**:
1. Establish the starting point
2. Trace key developments
3. Highlight turning points
4. Show current state of knowledge
5. Indicate future directions

### Pattern: Methodological Synthesis

**Use when**: Comparing how different studies approached a problem.

**Structure**:
1. Categorize by methodology
2. Compare strengths/weaknesses
3. Identify methodological gaps
4. Justify your chosen approach

### Pattern: Argumentative Synthesis

**Use when**: Building a case from multiple sources.

**Structure**:
1. State your thesis/claim
2. Present supporting evidence
3. Address counterarguments
4. Strengthen with additional evidence
5. Draw conclusions

## Evidence Quality Assessment

Rate each piece of evidence:

| Quality | Criteria | Usage |
|---------|----------|-------|
| **High** | Peer-reviewed, recent, directly relevant | Primary support |
| **Medium** | Reputable but older or tangential | Secondary support |
| **Low** | Non-peer-reviewed, outdated | Use cautiously or note limitations |

### Quality Checklist

- [ ] Is the source peer-reviewed?
- [ ] Is the publication reputable?
- [ ] Is the research recent (within 5-10 years)?
- [ ] Is the methodology sound?
- [ ] Are findings replicated elsewhere?
- [ ] Is there potential bias?

---

## Gap Identification

Actively look for:

1. **Topic gaps**: Areas not covered by existing sources
2. **Temporal gaps**: Recent developments missing
3. **Methodological gaps**: Approaches not represented
4. **Population gaps**: Groups not studied
5. **Geographical gaps**: Regions not covered

Document gaps explicitly:

```markdown
## Research Gaps Identified

1. **Gap**: No studies examine X in context Y
   - **Impact**: Limits applicability to our domain
   - **Action**: Flag for discussion in limitations

2. **Gap**: Most studies pre-date 2020
   - **Impact**: May miss recent developments
   - **Action**: Search for newer sources
```

## Citation Tracking

Maintain a citation log during synthesis:

```markdown
## Citation Log

### Used in Synthesis
- Smith (2023) - §2.1, §3.2
- Jones (2022) - §2.2
- Brown (2021) - §4.1

### Pending Review
- Davis (2020) - Need to extract quotes
- Miller (2019) - Relevance unclear

### Rejected
- Wilson (2018) - Superseded by Smith (2023)
```

## Output Format

Your synthesized document should include:

```markdown
# Research Synthesis: [Topic]

## Executive Summary
[2-3 paragraph overview of key findings]

## Themes

### Theme 1: [Name]
[Synthesized narrative with inline citations]

### Theme 2: [Name]
[Synthesized narrative with inline citations]

## Evidence Map

| Paper Section | Supporting Evidence | Confidence |
|---------------|---------------------|------------|
| ... | ... | High/Medium/Low |

## Gaps and Limitations
[List of identified gaps]

## Source Quality Summary
[Table of sources with quality ratings]

## Citations Used
[List of all citations for bibliography]
```

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Source-by-source summary** instead of thematic synthesis
2. **Dropping citations** during paraphrasing
3. **Over-relying** on a single source
4. **Ignoring contradictions** between sources
5. **Losing page numbers** for direct quotes
6. **Mixing synthesis with interpretation** (keep them separate)
