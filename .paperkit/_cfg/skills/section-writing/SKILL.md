# Academic Section Writing

## Your Task

When drafting a paper section, produce scholarly prose that:

1. **Follows** the conventions for that section type
2. **Flows** logically from paragraph to paragraph
3. **Integrates** evidence seamlessly with narrative
4. **Maintains** appropriate academic tone
5. **Connects** to adjacent sections

---

## Section-Specific Guidance

### Introduction

**Purpose**: Orient readers, establish significance, preview structure.

**Structure**:
1. **Hook** - Engaging opening that establishes relevance
2. **Context** - Background needed to understand the problem
3. **Gap/Problem** - What's missing or needs addressing
4. **Purpose** - What this paper aims to do
5. **Scope** - What's included and excluded
6. **Preview** - Brief roadmap of the paper

**Key Moves**:
- Start broad, narrow to specific
- Use present tense for general truths
- End with clear thesis or purpose statement

**Example Opening Patterns**:
```
"In recent years, [topic] has attracted increasing attention due to..."
"Despite extensive research on [topic], [gap] remains unexplored..."
"The challenge of [problem] has significant implications for..."
```

---

### Literature Review / Background

**Purpose**: Establish theoretical foundation, position your work.

**Structure**:
1. **Thematic Organization** - Group by topic, not chronologically
2. **Critical Analysis** - Don't just summarize, evaluate
3. **Synthesis** - Show connections between sources
4. **Gap Identification** - Lead to your contribution

**Key Moves**:
- Use reporting verbs (argues, demonstrates, suggests)
- Compare and contrast perspectives
- Build toward your research gap

**Transition Patterns**:
```
"Building on this foundation, [Author] extended..."
"In contrast to the view that..., [Author] argues..."
"These findings align with earlier work by..."
```

---

### Methodology / Methods

**Purpose**: Explain how you did the work so others can replicate.

**Structure**:
1. **Overview** - Brief summary of approach
2. **Materials/Participants** - What/who was involved
3. **Procedure** - Step-by-step process
4. **Analysis** - How data was processed
5. **Justification** - Why these choices

**Key Moves**:
- Use past tense for what you did
- Be precise and specific
- Justify choices with citations

**Precision Examples**:
```
✗ "We collected some data..."
✓ "Data were collected from 150 participants over a 6-week period..."

✗ "We analyzed the results..."
✓ "Thematic analysis was conducted following Braun and Clarke's (2006) six-phase approach..."
```

---

### Results / Findings

**Purpose**: Present what you found without interpretation.

**Structure**:
1. **Overview** - Summary of main findings
2. **Detailed Presentation** - Finding by finding
3. **Supporting Evidence** - Data, quotes, statistics
4. **Objective Description** - No interpretation yet

**Key Moves**:
- Use past tense
- Present findings in logical order
- Reference figures/tables explicitly
- Avoid interpretation (save for Discussion)

**Presenting Data**:
```
"As shown in Table 1, participants..."
"Figure 2 illustrates the relationship between..."
"Analysis revealed three primary themes: (1)..., (2)..., (3)..."
```

---

### Discussion

**Purpose**: Interpret findings, connect to literature, acknowledge limitations.

**Structure**:
1. **Summary** - Key findings restated
2. **Interpretation** - What findings mean
3. **Connection** - How this relates to existing literature
4. **Implications** - Why this matters
5. **Limitations** - Honest assessment of constraints
6. **Future Work** - Where to go from here

**Key Moves**:
- Connect each finding to literature
- Balance confidence with hedging
- Address unexpected results
- Be honest about limitations

**Interpretation Patterns**:
```
"These findings suggest that..."
"This result is consistent with [Author]'s (Year) observation that..."
"Contrary to expectations, the data indicate..."
"One possible explanation for this finding is..."
```

---

### Conclusion

**Purpose**: Synthesize, emphasize significance, close effectively.

**Structure**:
1. **Restatement** - Thesis/purpose reminder
2. **Summary** - Key points (not just repetition)
3. **Significance** - Why this matters
4. **Implications** - Practical or theoretical
5. **Final Thought** - Memorable closing

**Key Moves**:
- Don't introduce new information
- Elevate to broader significance
- End with impact

**Closing Patterns**:
```
"This study demonstrates that..."
"Taken together, these findings advance our understanding of..."
"As [field] continues to evolve, [contribution] provides..."
```

## Academic Writing Conventions

### Tone and Voice

| Preferred | Avoid |
|-----------|-------|
| Formal | Casual/colloquial |
| Objective | Emotional/subjective |
| Precise | Vague |
| Hedged (where appropriate) | Absolute claims |
| Active voice (often) | Excessive passive |

### Hedging Language

Use hedging for claims that aren't certain:

| Strong Claim | Hedged Version |
|--------------|----------------|
| This proves | This suggests |
| Always | Often, typically |
| Clearly | Arguably, apparently |
| Must | May, might, could |

### Transition Words

**Addition**: Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally, In addition
**Contrast**: However, Nevertheless, Conversely, In contrast
**Cause/Effect**: Therefore, Consequently, As a result, Thus
**Example**: For instance, For example, Specifically, To illustrate
**Sequence**: First, Subsequently, Finally, Meanwhile
**Summary**: In summary, Overall, To conclude, In brief

---

## Paragraph Structure

Each paragraph should:

1. **Topic Sentence** - Main point of the paragraph
2. **Development** - Evidence, examples, explanation
3. **Analysis** - Interpretation, significance
4. **Transition** - Link to next paragraph

**Example Structure**:
```
[Topic] Research has shown that color perception varies significantly across individuals.
[Evidence] Smith (2023) found that approximately 8% of males experience some form of color vision deficiency.
[Analysis] This variability has important implications for color journey design, as palettes must account for perceptual differences.
[Transition] Building on this understanding, the following section examines...
```

---

## Flow and Coherence

### Creating Flow

1. **Use transition words** at paragraph starts
2. **Repeat key terms** (but not excessively)
3. **Use pronouns** to link to previous sentences
4. **Follow old-to-new information** pattern

### Information Flow

**Old information** (what reader knows) → **New information** (what you're adding)

```
✓ "The algorithm processes input data. This data is first validated..."
✓ "Three factors influence perception. The most significant of these is..."
```

### Avoiding Choppiness

Connect short, related sentences:

```
✗ "The study was conducted. It involved 50 participants. They were students."
✓ "The study involved 50 student participants."
```

## Citation Integration

### Integral Citations (author as subject)

```
"Smith (2023) argues that..."
"According to Jones (2022),..."
"As Brown (2021) demonstrates,..."
```

### Non-Integral Citations (author in parentheses)

```
"Color perception varies across individuals (Smith, 2023)."
"Recent research supports this view (Jones, 2022; Brown, 2021)."
```

### Reporting Verbs (vary based on strength)

| Neutral | Tentative | Strong |
|---------|-----------|--------|
| states | suggests | argues |
| notes | implies | demonstrates |
| observes | indicates | proves |
| reports | proposes | establishes |

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Starting with definitions** - Weave definitions into context
2. **Listing without synthesis** - Connect and analyze sources
3. **Abrupt transitions** - Use linking phrases
4. **Inconsistent tense** - Match section conventions
5. **Over-hedging** - Balance caution with confidence
6. **Unsupported claims** - Every claim needs evidence or qualification

| Section | Tense | Voice | Key Focus |
|---------|-------|-------|-----------|
| Introduction | Present | Active | Engage, contextualize |
| Literature | Present/Past | Mix | Synthesize, critique |
| Methods | Past | Mix | Describe, justify |
| Results | Past | Mix | Present objectively |
| Discussion | Present/Past | Active | Interpret, connect |
| Conclusion | Present | Active | Synthesize, impact |
